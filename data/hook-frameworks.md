# Hook Frameworks

30 hook frameworks for short-form ads, written from scratch for this skill. A hook is the first
line or first 1-3 seconds — the part that decides whether anyone watches the rest.

Each framework is a reusable **structure**, not a fill-in-the-blank you paste verbatim. Swap the
`[bracketed]` slots for the real product, audience, and proof, then rewrite the wording so it
sounds like a person, not a template. Spread a test across **different families** (curiosity,
pain, proof, contrast, story…), not five rewordings of one idea.

## How to read this

- **Angle** = the strategic job (Problem / Solution / Product / Unaware).
- **Trigger** = the psychology it pulls (Curiosity, Pain, Proof, Authority, Contrast, Story,
  Urgency, Pattern, Identity, Mechanism, Confession, Myth).
- **Best format** = where it tends to perform (Video = short-form clip; Static = single image;
  Carousel = swipeable).
- **Awareness** = who it's for. Cold/Unaware needs the problem opened; Most-aware just needs a
  reason to act. See `data/angle-matrix.md`.

Never write a fake stat, quote, or result into these. If a proof slot has no real number, leave
it clearly marked for the user to fill.

## Family 1 — Curiosity & Open Loops

Open a gap the brain wants closed, then make watching the only way to close it.

### 1. The Held-Back Detail
**Template:** `There's one thing about [topic] that [category] keeps quiet. Here it is.`
Trigger: Curiosity · Angle: Solution · Best format: Video · Awareness: Cold
Opens a secret loop and promises the payoff is coming. Deliver it fast or you lose them.

### 2. The Quiet Discovery
**Template:** `Almost nobody is talking about [insight] yet. Give it two years and everybody will.`
Trigger: Curiosity · Angle: Solution · Best format: Video · Awareness: Cold
Positions the viewer as early. Works when the insight is genuinely non-obvious.

### 3. The Backwards Truth
**Template:** `Most people do [common approach] and wonder why it fails. It's built to fail. Do this instead.`
Trigger: Curiosity · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Cold
Reframes a familiar behavior as the actual cause of the problem.

### 4. The Oddly Specific Reason
**Template:** `The real reason your [outcome] isn't working comes down to [very specific, unexpected detail].`
Trigger: Curiosity · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Problem-aware
Specificity reads as insider knowledge. Vague hooks get scrolled; precise ones get watched.

### 5. The Interrupted Moment
**Template:** `I was halfway through [ordinary task] when I realized [unexpected thing]...`
Trigger: Curiosity · Angle: Unaware · Best format: Video · Awareness: Cold
A story that stops mid-sentence. The unfinished thought pulls the eye down the feed.

## Family 2 — Pain & Problem

Name the pain sharper than the viewer can name it themselves. Recognition earns attention.

### 6. The Loop They Know
**Template:** `You try [fix]. It works for a week. You're back where you started. Sound familiar?`
Trigger: Pain · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Problem-aware
Mirrors the exact frustration cycle. "Sound familiar?" makes them nod before you sell.

### 7. The Stacked Symptoms
**Template:** `[Symptom]? [Symptom]? [Symptom]? It isn't [what they blame]. It's [the real cause].`
Trigger: Pain · Angle: Unaware · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Cold
Three quick symptoms build a "that's me" moment, then you flip to the reframe.

### 8. The Running Tab
**Template:** `Every week you leave [problem] alone, it quietly costs you [specific consequence].`
Trigger: Pain · Angle: Problem · Best format: Static, Video · Awareness: Problem-aware
Makes inaction expensive. Keep the consequence concrete, never a made-up dollar figure.

### 9. The Not-Your-Fault
**Template:** `This was never your fault. [System / industry] set you up to struggle with [problem].`
Trigger: Pain · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video · Awareness: Problem-aware
Removes blame, which lowers the defensiveness that kills cold ads.

### 10. The Wrong Target
**Template:** `Stop pouring effort into [common fix]. The thing actually holding you back is [reframe].`
Trigger: Pain · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Solution-aware

## Family 3 — Proof & Results

Lead with the receipt. Works best when the audience already suspects a solution exists.

### 11. The Receipt First
**Template:** `[Real, specific result] in [timeframe]. No [common objection]. Here's exactly how.`
Trigger: Proof · Angle: Product · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Solution-aware
The number does the stopping; "here's exactly how" earns the watch. Use only real results.

### 12. The Split Screen
**Template:** `[Before state, in a vivid detail]. [Timeframe] later: [after state, specific].`
Trigger: Proof · Angle: Product · Best format: Video, Carousel · Awareness: Solution-aware
Contrast is instantly legible on mute. Show it, don't just say it.

### 13. The Head Count
**Template:** `[Real number] people already switched to [product]. Here's what they figured out.`
Trigger: Proof · Angle: Product · Best format: Static, Video · Awareness: Solution-aware
Social proof plus an open loop. Only use a count you can actually stand behind.

### 14. The Objection, Pre-Answered
**Template:** `"[The exact objection]"? I thought that too. Then [what changed my mind].`
Trigger: Proof · Angle: Product · Best format: Video · Awareness: Most-aware
Says the doubt out loud before they can, then dissolves it with proof.

## Family 4 — Authority & Credibility

Borrow trust from a role, an experience, or a track record.

### 15. The Insider Role
**Template:** `[Number] years in [industry] taught me one thing about [topic] most people never learn.`
Trigger: Authority · Angle: Solution · Best format: Video · Awareness: Cold
Credentials up front buy you the next five seconds. Keep the claim honest.

### 16. The Field Notes
**Template:** `We took [client type] from [before] to [after] in [timeframe]. Here's the playbook.`
Trigger: Authority · Angle: Solution · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Solution-aware
A case-study lead. The word "playbook" promises a repeatable method, not luck.

### 17. The Current Standard
**Template:** `This is how sharp [persona] handle [problem] now, not the way everyone did it three years ago.`
Trigger: Authority · Angle: Solution · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Solution-aware
Frames the old way as outdated and the viewer as capable of the new one.

## Family 5 — Contrast & Reframe

Set two things side by side so the better choice is obvious.

### 18. The Then vs. Now
**Template:** `The old way: [slow, painful process]. The new way: [your simple version].`
Trigger: Contrast · Angle: Solution · Best format: Static, Video · Awareness: Solution-aware
Cleanest structure in advertising. Make the old way feel genuinely tiring.

### 19. The Price Reframe
**Template:** `You'll happily spend [amount] on [forgettable thing]. This costs less and [real payoff].`
Trigger: Contrast · Angle: Product · Best format: Static, Video · Awareness: Most-aware
Anchors your price against something the viewer already wastes money on.

### 20. The Cost-of-Problem Flip
**Template:** `"Too pricey"? Add up what [problem] is already costing you every month. Now compare.`
Trigger: Contrast · Angle: Product · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Most-aware
Turns the price objection into a math problem the viewer loses.

## Family 6 — Story & Origin

A first-person arc the viewer can drop themselves into.

### 21. The Founder's Reason
**Template:** `I got tired of [relatable struggle], so I built [product]. Now [what changed].`
Trigger: Story · Angle: Solution · Best format: Video · Awareness: Cold
Origin stories humanize a product and pre-load trust. Keep it true.

### 22. The Year-Ago Me
**Template:** `A year ago I was exactly where you are with [problem]. One change moved everything.`
Trigger: Story · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video · Awareness: Problem-aware
The "I was you" bridge. The viewer sees their own future in your past.

### 23. The Tested-It-For-You
**Template:** `I ran [trend / method / product] as the guinea pig for [timeframe]. Here's the honest verdict.`
Trigger: Story · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video · Awareness: Cold
Positions you as the honest tester, which lowers ad-skepticism instantly.

## Family 7 — Urgency & Timing

A real reason to move now. Only use urgency that's true — fake countdowns burn trust.

### 24. The Calendar Nudge
**Template:** `[Season / event] is [timeframe] out. If you haven't [action] yet, this is the reminder.`
Trigger: Urgency · Angle: Product · Best format: Static, Video · Awareness: Most-aware
Ties action to a real date on the viewer's calendar rather than a fake scarcity clock.

### 25. The Permission to Start
**Template:** `You've researched enough. You've waited long enough. Here's your go-ahead to [action].`
Trigger: Urgency · Angle: Product · Best format: Static, Video · Awareness: Most-aware
For warm audiences who are already convinced and just need a push.

---

## Family 8 — Pattern Interrupt

Break the scroll rhythm with something that doesn't look like an ad.

### 26. The Cold Start
**Template:** `[Drop the viewer into the middle of an action or reaction with zero setup.]`
Trigger: Pattern · Angle: Unaware · Best format: Video · Awareness: Cold
No intro, no logo, no "hey guys." Motion mid-scene stops the thumb.

### 27. The Direct Address
**Template:** `[Specific persona], yes you, this one's actually for you. Don't scroll past.`
Trigger: Pattern · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Cold
Calls out one person so precisely that they feel seen. Narrow beats broad.

### 28. The Hot Take
**Template:** `Unpopular opinion: [a genuinely contrarian stance on the topic].`
Trigger: Pattern · Angle: Problem · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Cold
A real opinion earns a reaction; a safe one gets ignored. Mean it.

---

## Family 9 — Identity & Mechanism

Speak to who they are, or reveal the one thing that makes it work.

### 29. The Self-Select
**Template:** `If you're the kind of [persona] who [aspirational trait], keep watching.`
Trigger: Identity · Angle: Unaware · Best format: Video, Static · Awareness: Cold
Filters for the right buyer and flatters them into staying.

### 30. The One Lever
**Template:** `It works because of one thing almost every [category] ignores: [the mechanism].`
Trigger: Mechanism · Angle: Solution · Best format: Video · Awareness: Solution-aware
Names a unique mechanism, which makes the product feel like more than a me-too.

## Using these well

- **One hook, one job.** Don't stack three triggers into one line. Pick the sharpest.
- **Specific beats clever.** A real number or a concrete detail out-pulls a pun.
- **First second is visual too.** On video, the on-screen text and the opening frame are half the
  hook — see `references/platform-notes.md`.
- **Test across families.** If your winners are all curiosity, you don't know if curiosity is
  winning or if it's the only thing you tested.
