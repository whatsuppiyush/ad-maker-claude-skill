# Angle × Awareness Matrix

The single most useful decision before writing a hook: **which angle, for which awareness stage.**
Get this wrong and even a perfect hook falls flat, because it's answering a question the viewer
isn't asking yet.

## The four angles

| Angle | What it leads with | When it wins |
|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | The problem, named sharply | Audience feels the problem but hasn't acted |
| **Desire** | The dream outcome / after-state | Audience is motivated by the upside, not the fear |
| **Proof** | Results, numbers, social proof | Audience believes solutions exist but doubts yours |
| **Curiosity** | An open loop / surprising claim | Cold audience with no reason to care yet |

## The five awareness stages (Eugene Schwartz)

How much the prospect already knows shapes what the hook must do.

| Stage | They know... | The hook's job |
|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Nothing — not even that they have the problem | Interrupt, then reveal the problem exists |
| **Problem-aware** | They have the problem, not that solutions exist | Agitate the problem, hint a way out |
| **Solution-aware** | Solutions exist, not that yours is best | Differentiate — proof, mechanism, contrast |
| **Product-aware** | Your product, but they haven't bought | Handle objections, show the specific win |
| **Most-aware** | Everything — they just need a nudge | Reason to act now: offer, urgency, permission |

## The matrix — which angle fits which stage

`★` = strong fit, `○` = works with care, blank = usually wrong for this stage.

| Angle ↓ / Stage → | Unaware | Problem-aware | Solution-aware | Product-aware | Most-aware |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pain** | ★ | ★ | ○ | | |
| **Desire** | ○ | ★ | ★ | ○ | ○ |
| **Proof** | | ○ | ★ | ★ | ★ |
| **Curiosity** | ★ | ★ | ○ | | |

**How to read it:** a cold, unaware placement (broad prospecting) wants Pain or Curiosity. A
retargeting placement full of people who already visited the site is Product- or Most-aware —
give them Proof and a reason to act, not a problem they've already accepted.

## Filling the matrix for one product (what `/ad-angles` produces)

For a given product, write **one hook per useful cell** so the user has a message for every stage
of the funnel:

1. **Unaware / Curiosity** — the surprising claim that makes a stranger stop.
2. **Problem-aware / Pain** — the exact frustration, mirrored back.
3. **Solution-aware / Proof** — why yours, with a real result or mechanism.
4. **Product-aware / Proof** — the objection killed, the specific win shown.
5. **Most-aware / Desire or Urgency** — the offer and the nudge.

That's a full-funnel set from one product brief — cold hook to closing nudge — instead of five
hooks that all target the same warm sliver of the audience.

## Rules

- **Never** write a Most-aware hook (offer/urgency) for a cold, Unaware placement — they don't
  know you exist yet.
- **Match the placement, not your excitement.** Prospecting = top of the matrix. Retargeting =
  bottom.
- **One angle per ad.** A hook that tries to do pain + proof + urgency does none of them.
