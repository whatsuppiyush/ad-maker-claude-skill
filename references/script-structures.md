# Script Structures — hook → body → CTA

A short-form ad has three jobs in order: **stop the scroll (hook), pay off the promise (body),
ask for one thing (CTA).** The hook gets the frameworks in `data/hook-frameworks.md`; this file
is the structure for everything after it.

## The universal spine

1. **Hook (0-3s)** — earn the next three seconds. One idea, the sharpest one.
2. **Body (the middle)** — deliver on the hook. Show the mechanism, the proof, the transformation
   — whatever the hook promised. This is where you actually sell.
3. **CTA (last 2-4s)** — one clear action. Not three. One.

Keep the whole thing tight: most winning short-form ads run **15-40 seconds**. If a line doesn't
move the viewer toward the CTA, cut it.

## Five body structures (pick by product and angle)

### 1. Problem → Agitate → Solve (PAS)
Best for pain-led angles and cold audiences.
- **Hook:** name the problem (Family 2).
- **Agitate:** make it vivid — the cost, the frustration, the loop they're stuck in.
- **Solve:** introduce the product as the specific way out, then the CTA.
Watch-out: agitate briefly. Over-agitating on short-form loses the viewer before the solve.

### 2. Before → After → Bridge (BAB)
Best for desire-led angles and visible transformations.
- **Hook:** the before-state, in a vivid detail (Family 3, The Split Screen).
- **After:** paint the outcome — specific, real.
- **Bridge:** the product is how you get from one to the other. CTA.

### 3. UGC / testimonial
Best for social proof and native-feeling placements.
- **Hook:** a real-sounding first-person line ("I did not expect this to work...").
- **Body:** the honest arc — skeptical, tried it, here's what happened, here's the detail I
  didn't expect. Show the product in use.
- **CTA:** soft and personal ("link's in the bio, try it").
See `references/ugc-playbook.md` for the full brief.

### 4. Founder / origin
Best for brand trust and newer products.
- **Hook:** the reason you built it (Family 6, The Founder's Reason).
- **Body:** the struggle → the fix → what's different about how you do it (the mechanism).
- **CTA:** invite them to be part of it.

### 5. Listicle / rapid-fire
Best for feature-rich products and demos.
- **Hook:** "[Number] things about [product] that [surprising frame]."
- **Body:** fast cuts, one point per cut, on-screen text per point, each with a micro-payoff.
- **CTA:** the round-up line + one action.

## Writing the body well

- **Keep the hook's promise front-of-mind.** If the hook said "here's exactly how," the body must
  actually show how — fast. A broken promise is why watch-time craters at second four.
- **One point per beat.** Short-form doesn't allow subordinate clauses. One idea, cut, next idea.
- **Show, don't claim.** "It's easy" is a claim. Footage of it being easy is proof.
- **Proof goes in the body, not the hook** (unless proof *is* the hook). A real number, a real
  demo, a real result — never invented.
- **Sound and text carry it.** Assume mute: on-screen text must let the ad make sense with no
  audio. Assume sound-on too: a trending sound or a confident VO lifts retention.

## Writing the CTA well

- **One action.** "Comment WORD, click the link, and follow" is three asks and gets zero. Pick
  the one that matters.
- **Make it concrete.** Not "learn more" — "tap the link and start your first [thing] free."
- **Match the platform** — see `references/platform-notes.md`. TikTok/Reels lean "link in bio";
  Meta feed uses the CTA button + a direct line.
- **Lower the risk** at the moment of the ask: free trial, guarantee, "takes two minutes," "no
  card." Only promise what's true.
- **Give a reason to act now** for warm audiences (Family 7), but skip fake countdowns.

## A quick quality pass before shipping

- Does the first line work with the sound off?
- Could the hook front any competitor's ad, or only this product? (If any competitor, sharpen it.)
- Is there exactly one CTA?
- Did any number, quote, or result get invented? (If yes, cut or mark it for a real figure.)
- Read it out loud — does it sound like a person or a brochure?
