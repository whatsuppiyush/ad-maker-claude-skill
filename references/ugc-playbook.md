# UGC Playbook — briefing a creator so the footage comes back usable

UGC (user-generated-content-style) ads are shot by a creator on a phone to look like a real person
talking, not a produced commercial. They're the highest-converting format on TikTok and Reels for
one reason: they don't look like ads. The whole game is won or lost in the **brief.** A vague
brief comes back as unusable footage; a tight brief comes back ready to edit.

## What a good brief contains

1. **The one-line concept** — what this specific video is about, in a sentence. ("A skeptical
   first-time user tries [product] for a week and shows the result.")
2. **The hook** — the exact opening line and the opening action, written out. Don't leave the hook
   to chance; it's the whole ad. Give 2-3 hook options to shoot.
3. **Talking points** — the beats to hit, in order, in the creator's own words. Bullet points, not
   a word-for-word script (a memorized script reads stiff and kills the "real person" effect).
4. **The shot list** — the specific shots you need cut together (see below).
5. **Do / Don't** — the guardrails that keep it on-brand and compliant.
6. **The vibe** — energy, tone, pace, references ("like you're telling your friend, fast and a
   little surprised"). Link a reference video if you have one.
7. **The CTA** — the exact closing line and action.
8. **Logistics** — aspect ratio (9:16), rough length, lighting (face a window), audio (quiet
   room), how many hook variations, and delivery format (raw clips vs. edited).

## Writing talking points (not a script)

Give the creator the *arc* and let them phrase it naturally:
- **Hook** — the scroll-stopper (from `data/hook-frameworks.md`).
- **The turn** — why they were skeptical / what problem they had.
- **The try** — using the product, shown on camera.
- **The payoff** — the specific result or moment it clicked.
- **The recommend** — why they'd tell a friend.
- **The CTA** — where to get it.

Tell them: say it like you're texting a friend, stumble a little, keep it real. Polish is the
enemy of UGC.

## The shot list a creator can actually follow

Spell out the distinct shots so the edit has coverage. A typical UGC ad needs:
- **Hook shot** — talking to camera, or an attention-grabbing action, first 1-3s. Shoot the 2-3
  hook variations here.
- **B-roll of the problem** — the frustration, the mess, the before.
- **Product reveal** — unboxing / first look / the product in hand.
- **Product in use** — the key shots showing it work. This is the proof; get plenty.
- **Reaction / result** — the genuine "oh, this actually works" moment.
- **CTA shot** — talking to camera, holding the product, saying where to get it.

Ask for **more coverage than you think you need** — extra B-roll saves an edit; missing B-roll
kills one.

## Do / Don't guardrails

**Do:**
- Shoot vertical, 9:16, phone held steady or on a small tripod.
- Face a natural light source.
- Record in a quiet room; the audio matters as much as the video.
- Show the product actually being used, clearly.
- Shoot multiple hook takes — the hook is what gets swapped in testing.

**Don't:**
- Don't read a script word-for-word (it reads fake).
- Don't make claims that aren't true — no invented results, no "cures," no fake before/afters.
- Don't over-produce it: no studio lighting, no ad-voice, no logo splash intro.
- Don't crowd the edges with key text/action — respect the safe zones
  (`references/platform-notes.md`).
- Don't use copyrighted music the brand can't license.

## Why authenticity converts

The viewer's brain is running an "is this an ad?" filter every second of the feed. UGC works
because it slips under that filter — a real face, a real room, a real voice, an honest arc. The
moment it feels produced, the filter flips on and the thumb moves. Every choice in the brief —
handheld, natural light, unscripted phrasing, genuine reaction — protects that realness. Brief
for real, not for polished.

## Compliance quick-check

Before the brief goes out, make sure it asks for nothing untrue: no fabricated testimonials, no
health/income claims the brand can't back, no "results not typical" territory without the honesty
to match. Honest UGC is both the compliant choice and the higher-converting one — audiences smell
a staged testimonial.
